# Vision & Mission

## Mission

Equip caregivers of children and teens with suspected or diagnosed mood disorders with a structured, longitudinal observation tool that turns daily chaos into clinician-ready data — without adding emotional friction to an already difficult life.

## Vision

A world where no caregiver walks into a clinician's office and says "I don't know how to describe what's been happening." Storm Tracker makes the invisible visible: patterns emerge from daily observations, cycles become predictable, and caregivers gain confidence that what they're seeing is real, measurable, and worth discussing.

## Core Values

### 1. The app is a safe space.

Not just a tracking tool — a place a parent goes after a hard day with their teen. The emotional experience of using the app matters as much as the functionality. Every design decision should reduce friction, not add it.

### 2. Validate the caregiver's experience.

"It's not all in your head." Parents of children with mood disorders are often gaslit by systems, schools, and sometimes their own families. This app takes their observations seriously, quantifies them, and presents them in a way that commands clinical attention.

### 3. Never diagnose. Always inform.

Storm Tracker is an observation tool, not a diagnostic instrument. It organizes and quantifies — it never concludes. Language throughout the app uses "possible," "pattern observed," "signal" — never "diagnosis," "confirmed," or "likely." All interpretation is clinician-led.

### 4. See the whole child.

Tracking only the hard days creates a distorted picture. The app must also capture good days, normal behavior, and positive moments so clinicians see baseline, not just crisis. Caregivers need to be reminded that good days exist.

### 5. Meet caregivers where they are.

A parent at the end of their rope at 11pm needs to log in 30 seconds. A parent preparing for a psychiatrist appointment needs a detailed report. The app serves both modes — quick log for survival, detailed entry for depth.

### 6. Evidence-based, not opinion-based.

All behavioral tracking maps to DSM-5 diagnostic criteria. Scoring, classification, and episode detection follow published clinical frameworks. The app doesn't invent its own psychology — it operationalizes existing clinical science for parent use.

### 7. Deeply personal, not generic.

The app should feel like *their* app. Project theming, customization, and a personal touch matter. "I want them to love using it, not go 'oh I've got to track my thing.'"

### 8. Faith-informed option.

The creator's worldview includes a theology of mental illness and neurodivergence. Faith-based encouragement and resources should be available as an opt-in experience, never imposed.

## Target Users

### Primary: The Exhausted Caregiver

A parent (usually a mother) of a child or teen exhibiting mood disorder symptoms. She may or may not have a diagnosis yet. She's been told "all kids are moody" and she knows something is different. She needs:
- Validation that her observations are real
- A way to communicate what she's seeing to clinicians
- Pattern recognition she can't do in her head
- Encouragement on the hard days

### Secondary: The Clinician

A psychiatrist, psychologist, or therapist who receives the caregiver's report. They need:
- Structured, longitudinal data (not "he's been having a rough month")
- DSM-aligned behavioral observations
- Frequency counts and trend visualization
- Enough context to accelerate diagnosis or adjust treatment

### Tertiary: The Teen (Self-Tracker)

An older teen who joins their own project to log self-observations alongside caregiver logs. Their data is tagged as self-reported. Discrepancies between caregiver and teen observations are clinically valuable.

## Positioning

**What it is:** A parent-friendly, DSM-aligned, longitudinal behavioral observation tool.

**What it isn't:** A diagnostic tool, a treatment recommender, a replacement for clinical care.

**Competitive frame:** There is no direct competitor doing this. Mood tracking apps (Daylio, eMoods) track the user's own mood. Storm Tracker tracks *someone else's* observable behavior and maps it to clinical criteria. It's built for the observer, not the patient.
